What Short Study covers, and how we teach it

Short Study was built around a simple idea: a student preparing for a Class 8, 9 or 10 exam — or a beginner trying to learn their first programming language — shouldn't have to choose between "free" and "actually good." Every resource on this site is written from scratch, organised chapter by chapter, and reviewed against the current syllabus, so what you're reading is both free and genuinely usable for exam preparation.

Curriculum coverage, class by class

Our Class 8 notes lay the groundwork for the two board-prep years that follow — covering Mathematics (rational numbers, linear equations, mensuration), Science (crop production, cell structure, force and pressure), Social Science, and language grammar for English and Hindi. Class 9 notes build directly on that foundation with number systems, polynomials, and coordinate geometry in Maths, along with matter, tissues, and motion in Science — the exact chapters that reappear, in harder form, on the Class 10 board exam. Class 10 notes are written specifically with board exams in mind: real numbers, trigonometry, and quadratic equations in Maths; chemical reactions, life processes, and electricity in Science; plus a dedicated IT (Code 402) previous-year-questions resource with detailed, examiner-style solutions.

Alongside school notes, our Programming section is built for absolute beginners and freshers preparing for entry-level technical roles. It starts with Python fundamentals, moves through C and Java for a deeper understanding of how code actually executes, covers HTML & CSS for anyone interested in web development, and finishes with Data Structures and SQL basics — the exact topics that come up most often in fresher-level coding interviews.

Our teaching methodology

Every note on Short Study follows the same underlying approach, regardless of subject: understand first, memorise second. Instead of dense textbook paragraphs, our notes lead with a plain-language explanation of what a concept actually means, followed by worked examples, and finish with the kind of concise, highlighted summary points you'd want in front of you the night before an exam. For Mathematics and Science, that means step-by-step worked numericals rather than just final answers. For Social Science and languages, it means short, memorable revision points instead of full paragraphs you have to re-read every time. For programming, it means building the mental model behind a concept — what a loop actually does, why a linked list is useful — before showing the code that implements it.

We also deliberately keep our notes handwritten rather than typed. This isn't just an aesthetic choice — writing (and reading well-structured handwriting) engages memory differently than plain typed text does, and it mirrors the actual answer-sheet format that board exams reward: clear structure, underlined key terms, and diagrams labelled the way a well-prepared student would draw them.

Yes — every note, chapter, and programming resource on this site is free to view and download, with no sign-up, login, or hidden payment. There is no premium tier and there never will be.

We cover Class 8, Class 9 and Class 10 across Mathematics, Science, Social Science, English and Hindi, plus a dedicated Programming section covering Python, C, Java, HTML/CSS, Data Structures and SQL basics for beginners and freshers.

Are the notes updated for the latest syllabus?

Yes — we regularly review our notes against the current CBSE syllabus and question patterns, and update chapters when something changes rather than leaving them untouched for years.

Start with our Python notes in the Programming section — its simple syntax makes it one of the easiest languages to build confidence in early, and our notes assume zero prior coding background.

Can I download notes to study offline?

Yes — every note is available to view online or download, so you can study offline whenever you don't have a reliable connection.
