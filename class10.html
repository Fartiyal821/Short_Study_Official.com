Your complete guide to Class 10 board exam notes

Class 10 is the first board exam most CBSE students face, and it changes how everything needs to be studied. It's no longer enough to understand a chapter — you need to be able to reproduce it clearly, under time pressure, in the exact format board examiners expect. That shift, from "I understand this" to "I can write this out perfectly in 3 minutes," is what our Class 10 notes are built around.

What the Class 10 syllabus covers

Our Class 10 notes are organised chapter-wise across every core subject. Mathematics notes cover real numbers, polynomials, pairs of linear equations, and trigonometry — high-weightage chapters where step-marking matters as much as the final answer. Science notes span chemical reactions and equations, life processes, light, and electricity, broken down with the exact diagrams and derivations board papers expect. Social Science covers power sharing, resources and development, and nationalism, while our English notes consolidate grammar and the First Flight literature chapters into board-ready answer formats. We also offer a dedicated IT (Code 402) resource — a Previous Year Questions collection with detailed, examiner-style solutions covering six years of board papers.

Why handwritten notes work better for board prep

Board exams reward answers that look the way a well-prepared student's answer sheet looks — clean structure, underlined key terms, and diagrams drawn with labels in the right place. That's exactly the format our handwritten notes follow, so revising from them doubles as practice for how you should actually write in the exam itself. It's a small difference that adds up across dozens of questions on exam day.

Study tips for Class 10 board exams

  • Start with high-weightage chapters in Maths and Science — real numbers, trigonometry, chemical reactions, and life processes consistently carry the most marks.
  • Practice writing full answers by hand under a timer, not just reading through notes — board exams test speed as much as knowledge.
  • Use our IT (402) PYQ notes to identify repeated question patterns; boards often reuse question structures year after year.
  • Revise Social Science using short recall points rather than full paragraphs — you can expand them into full answers during the exam itself.
  • Keep a separate "mistakes" notebook from your mock tests and revisit it in the final two weeks before the exam.
