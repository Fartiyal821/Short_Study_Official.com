About Short Study

The story behind Short Study

Why a free notes platform exists, who it's built for, and what we're trying to prove.

How it started

Notes shouldn't cost money to be good

Short Study started from a simple, slightly frustrating observation: the best study notes online were almost always locked behind a payment, a subscription, or an app download — while the free ones were usually scanned, disorganised, or missing half the chapters. Students who could pay got a smooth experience. Students who couldn't were left piecing together PDFs from a dozen different sources, half of them unreadable.

That gap didn't make sense to us. A good lesson doesn't cost anything to produce beyond time and care — it's not a scarce resource that needs a price tag. So we started writing our own: full written lessons, organised topic by topic the way a genuinely well-structured course would be, and posted for free, with no login wall in the way.

That's where Short Study's programming curriculum started: written lessons on Python, C, Java, HTML/CSS, Data Structures and SQL, each built to be read start to finish rather than skimmed for a single snippet — free, with no login wall, and no premium tier hiding the good parts.

Where we're headed

Our vision for Short Study

We're a small team, and we like it that way — it keeps the site honest and directly accountable to the people using it. Right now, that means most of our energy goes into two things: writing more programming lessons, and making sure the ones already published are actually correct and useful.

Longer term, our goal is for Short Study to be the first place a complete beginner opens when they decide to try coding for the first time, and a genuinely useful reference for freshers preparing for technical interviews. We'd rather publish six lessons that are fully written, reviewed, and correct than sixty half-finished pages — so we're building the curriculum deliberately, topic by topic, rather than rushing out content that isn't good enough to trust.

If you're using these lessons: thank you for being the reason this exists. If you ever spot something wrong, missing, or confusing, our Contact Us page goes straight to a real inbox, and we read everything that comes through it.

Content credibility

How lessons are written and verified

Transparency about where this content comes from and how it's checked.

✍️

Written from working knowledge

Lessons are written by contributors with hands-on programming experience in the language or topic being taught, not generated from a single source and republished.

🧪

Every code example is run

Code examples on this site are executed before publishing, and the output shown on each lesson page is the actual result of running that exact code.

🔍

Reviewed before publishing

A second pass checks each lesson for technical accuracy and clarity before it goes live — nothing is published as a first draft.

📅

Dated and periodically revisited

Every lesson displays a "Last Reviewed" date. When language conventions, tooling, or interview expectations shift, the lesson is revisited and updated.

We don't claim formal academic credentials for every contributor, and we think pretending otherwise would be dishonest. What we do commit to is accuracy: every code example is tested, every lesson is reviewed by someone other than its author before publishing, and corrections reported through our Contact Us page are investigated and applied. If you find a technical error anywhere on this site, we want to know about it — and we'll fix it.
